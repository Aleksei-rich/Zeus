**Meta description:** A practical guide to granite countertop care, including everyday cleaning, spill response, heat precautions, sealing considerations and what to avoid.

Granite is a natural stone, so its appearance and characteristics vary from slab to slab. Good everyday habits can help preserve the surface and reduce staining, scratching and edge damage.

## Everyday cleaning

For routine cleaning, use a soft cloth or non-abrasive sponge with warm water and a mild cleaner that is appropriate for natural stone. Remove residue and dry the surface rather than leaving standing water around seams, edges and fixtures.

Avoid abrasive pads and powders that can affect the polished finish. When trying a new cleaning product, follow the stone or product supplier's instructions.

## Respond to spills quickly

Granite can vary in porosity. Wiping spills promptly is a sensible precaution, especially with oils, strongly colored liquids and acidic foods. Blot the spill, clean the area with a stone-compatible product and dry it.

## Does granite need to be sealed?

Some granite installations benefit from a penetrating sealer, but not every stone or installation has the same requirements. The appropriate sealing schedule depends on the specific material and sealer. Ask the fabricator or stone supplier what was used and follow the product instructions rather than relying on a universal calendar.

## Heat and heavy cookware

Granite itself tolerates heat better than many household materials, but countertop assemblies also include seams, adhesives and nearby components. Using trivets for very hot cookware is a simple precaution and also protects surrounding surfaces.

## Cutting directly on granite

Use a cutting board. It protects knife edges, keeps food preparation more sanitary and reduces the chance of scratching or marking the countertop surface or finish.

## Protect edges and overhangs

Stone edges can chip if struck by a heavy object. Avoid standing or sitting on countertop overhangs. Large overhangs may require structural support depending on their dimensions and the installation design.

## Watch seams and sink areas

Keep an eye on countertop seams, caulk around sinks and areas that remain wet. If a seam changes noticeably, a sink becomes loose, or water is reaching the cabinet below, have the installation inspected rather than masking the problem with additional cleaner or caulk.

## Before choosing granite

When selecting a slab, look at the actual stone whenever possible because natural variation is part of the material. Ask about finish, maintenance, seam placement, edge profile and whether the selected stone requires sealing.

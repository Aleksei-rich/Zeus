# How to Care for Kitchen Cabinets

Kitchen cabinets deal with fingerprints, cooking residue, moisture, heat and frequent contact every day. A simple maintenance routine can help preserve the finish and catch small problems before they become larger repairs.

## Start with the gentlest cleaning method

For routine cleaning, begin with a soft microfiber cloth lightly dampened with water. If water alone is not enough, use a small amount of mild dish soap diluted in water. Wipe the surface gently, then remove soap residue with a clean damp cloth and dry the cabinet with a soft towel.

Avoid treating every cabinet finish the same way. Painted wood, stained wood, laminate, thermofoil and other finishes can react differently to cleaners. When a manufacturer supplies care instructions, those instructions should take priority.

## Do not leave water on cabinet surfaces

Water around sinks, dishwashers and coffee stations should be wiped up promptly. Pay particular attention to door edges, seams, toe kicks and the cabinet directly below the sink. Long exposure to moisture can affect finishes and, depending on cabinet construction, the underlying material.

## Remove cooking grease before it builds up

Cabinets near the cooktop and range hood usually need more frequent attention. A light cleaning done regularly is preferable to aggressive scrubbing after residue has hardened. Avoid abrasive pads and harsh cleaners unless the cabinet manufacturer specifically approves them.

## Protect cabinets from heat and steam

Use the range hood when cooking and avoid directing steam from countertop appliances toward cabinet doors or finished panels. After using appliances that create significant steam, check nearby cabinet surfaces for condensation and dry them if needed.

## Check hinges, pulls and drawer hardware

Loose handles can damage the finish around mounting holes if they continue moving. Periodically check pulls and knobs and tighten them carefully. If a door begins rubbing another door or a drawer no longer closes correctly, the hardware may need adjustment rather than force.

## Clean spills quickly

Coffee, sauces, oils and strongly colored foods are easier to remove when addressed promptly. Blot or wipe the spill rather than allowing it to dry on the finish.

## Avoid common cabinet-cleaning mistakes

Do not soak cabinet doors, use abrasive scouring pads, scrape residue with metal tools, or assume a strong household cleaner is safe for every finish. Test any unfamiliar product in an inconspicuous area first.

## A simple maintenance schedule

Wipe obvious splashes as they happen. Clean high-touch areas such as pulls and frequently used doors regularly. Inspect the sink cabinet and areas near appliances for moisture. Every few months, check hardware alignment and look for early signs of finish or water damage.

# How to Care for Granite Countertops

Granite is a natural stone, and everyday care should focus on keeping the surface clean while avoiding products or habits that can damage the finish or leave difficult residue.

## Everyday cleaning

Use a soft cloth or non-abrasive sponge with water and a cleaner appropriate for natural stone. Remove crumbs and grit before wiping so particles are not dragged across the surface. Drying the countertop after cleaning can reduce streaks and water marks.

## Respond to spills promptly

Natural stone can vary in porosity. Oils, strongly colored liquids and other spills should be cleaned promptly rather than left on the countertop. Blotting a spill can be preferable to spreading it across a larger area.

## Be careful with acidic and abrasive cleaners

Products containing acids or harsh abrasives may affect some stone surfaces or finishes. Do not assume a bathroom cleaner, descaler or general-purpose heavy-duty cleaner is appropriate for granite. Follow the stone or sealer manufacturer's instructions.

## Sealing is not a one-size-fits-all schedule

Some granite installations may benefit from a penetrating sealer, but the need and frequency depend on the specific stone, finish, existing treatment and product used. Instead of following a universal calendar, use the recommendations for the installed material and sealer.

## Heat and impact

Granite is durable, but a countertop system also includes seams, edges, adhesives and surrounding cabinetry. Using trivets for very hot cookware is a simple precaution. Avoid dropping heavy objects on edges and corners, where impact can be concentrated.

## Cutting directly on granite

A cutting board protects both the countertop finish and knife edges and keeps food preparation easier to manage. It also reduces the chance of dragging grit or hard particles across the polished surface.

## Watch the areas around sinks and seams

Keep silicone joints and sink areas clean and inspect them periodically. If a joint separates or water begins reaching cabinetry below, address the source rather than repeatedly wiping only the visible surface.

# How to Care for Quartz Countertops

Engineered quartz countertops are designed for everyday residential use, but they still benefit from sensible cleaning and protection from excessive heat, harsh chemicals and impact.

## Routine cleaning

For ordinary messes, use a soft cloth or non-abrasive sponge with water and a mild cleaner approved for the quartz brand. Wipe away residue and dry the surface if needed to prevent streaking.

## Clean spills before they dry

Food residue is usually easier to remove while fresh. For dried material, soften it with an appropriate cleaner rather than immediately reaching for an abrasive pad or sharp metal scraper.

## Protect quartz from high heat

Quartz surfaces contain resins in addition to mineral content. Use trivets or heat pads under hot cookware and heat-producing countertop appliances. Do not rely on the appearance of stone as proof that direct high heat is harmless.

## Avoid harsh chemical exposure

Strong solvents, highly alkaline or acidic chemicals, paint removers and other aggressive products may affect the surface. Follow the specific quartz manufacturer's care guidance when choosing a cleaner.

## Use cutting boards

Cutting boards help protect the finish and prevent unnecessary contact between knives, food and the countertop. They also make cleanup simpler.

## Treat edges and cutouts with care

Edges, sink openings and cooktop cutouts can be more vulnerable to concentrated impact than the middle of a slab. Avoid striking these areas with heavy cookware or tools.

## Manufacturer instructions matter

Quartz formulations and finishes vary by brand and collection. If the fabricator or manufacturer supplied written care instructions, keep them with your project records and use them as the primary reference.

Kitchen cabinets deal with fingerprints, cooking residue, moisture and daily wear. A simple maintenance routine can help keep cabinet doors, drawer fronts and boxes looking clean while reducing avoidable damage.

## Start with the gentlest cleaning method

For routine cleaning, begin with a soft microfiber cloth dampened with water. If water alone is not enough, use a small amount of mild dish soap diluted in warm water. Wipe the surface gently, then remove any soap residue with a second damp cloth and dry the area with a clean towel.

Avoid soaking cabinet surfaces. Water should not be allowed to sit around door edges, seams, joints or unfinished areas.

## Clean spills promptly

Cooking splashes, coffee, sauces and other spills are easier to remove when they are fresh. Blot or wipe the spill rather than allowing it to dry on the cabinet finish. Pay particular attention to cabinets near the sink, dishwasher, cooktop and trash pull-out, where moisture and residue are more common.

## Be careful with aggressive cleaners

Abrasive powders, scouring pads and harsh solvents can dull or scratch a cabinet finish. Before using a specialty cleaner, check the cabinet manufacturer's care instructions. If you are unsure whether a product is compatible with the finish, test it on a small, inconspicuous area first.

## Control moisture and steam

Repeated exposure to moisture can affect cabinet components and finishes. Use the kitchen exhaust system when cooking and avoid leaving wet towels hanging over cabinet doors. After a plumbing leak or dishwasher overflow, dry cabinet surfaces and nearby seams promptly and investigate the source of the water.

## Do not ignore hinges and drawer hardware

Doors that become misaligned or drawers that stop closing normally should be inspected rather than forced. Loose screws may need tightening, and adjustable hinges can sometimes be realigned. Soft-close hardware should move smoothly without requiring excessive force.

## Protect high-use areas

The cabinet under the sink is especially vulnerable to unnoticed leaks. A removable under-sink mat or tray can make small leaks easier to spot and help protect the cabinet floor. Periodically check plumbing connections and the area around the garbage disposal if one is installed.

## What about painted, laminate and wood finishes?

Different finishes can have different care requirements. Painted surfaces may show abrasion from aggressive scrubbing, while laminate surfaces still need protection around seams and edges. Natural wood and wood veneer can react differently to moisture and cleaning products. Manufacturer-specific instructions should take priority whenever they are available.

## A simple maintenance routine

For most kitchens, regular light cleaning is better than waiting until heavy grease buildup requires stronger cleaning. Wipe high-touch areas as needed, inspect sink-base cabinetry for moisture, and periodically check hinges, drawer slides and pulls.

If you are planning new cabinetry, ask about the finish, cabinet-box material, hardware and recommended care before installation. Those details can make long-term maintenance easier.
